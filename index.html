Section 01  ·  The Control Point

Intelligent capability can produce an output.
ADS-Ω™ asks whether a consequential action is validly formed, bounded, authorized, and reconstructable when it crosses into external effect.

// 01
Decision Identity
DCE™
A governed decision begins as a distinct instance with identity, class, source, authority context, and applicable policy binding.
A tool invocation is not itself a decision identity.
// 02
Decision Context & Basis
CIF™ + RVL™
Context is evaluated for provenance, freshness, integrity, and admissibility; observable decision-basis artifacts are evaluated for context, policy, purpose, and drift.
Context Trust is not truth. Private chain-of-thought is not required as a universal runtime control.
// 03
Risk, Authority & Constraints
AVC™ + Decision Constraint Envelope
Risk, uncertainty, thresholds, cumulative exposure, authority requirements, and effective execution constraints remain explicit before authorization.
Acceptable risk does not automatically mean authorization.
// 04
Authorization, Evidence & Containment
EG™ + TMG™ + CCF™
Authorization is enforced at the Decision Trust Boundary, evidence continuity supports reconstruction, and material change can trigger actual restriction and recovery.
A prior GRANT is not permanent trust.
The control point is not simply whether the system can act.
It is whether this decision is still permitted to act now, here, on this target, under these conditions.

Section 02  ·  The Seven Core Control Layers

Exactly seven layers. No eighth layer.

CONTROL LAYER 01
+
DCE
Decision Core Engine
Establishes the governed decision instance: identity, class, source, authority context, and applicable policy binding.
Owns decision initialization and identity.
Why it exists
A consequential action needs a durable decision anchor. DCE™ distinguishes the decision from the model output, tool call, execution event, and observed outcome.
CONTROL LAYER 02
+
CIF
Context Integrity Field
Evaluates context provenance, freshness, integrity, admissibility, conflict, and trust for the intended decision use.
Invariant Context Trust ≠ truth.
Privacy-aware admission
Sensitive context may be minimized, masked, tokenized, encrypted, retained by protected reference, or rejected before durable evidence binding.
CONTROL LAYER 03
+
RVL
Reasoning Validation Layer
Evaluates observable decision-basis artifacts for consistency with admitted context, policy, intended purpose, and detectable reasoning drift.
Surface observable Reasoning Artifacts.
Why it exists
ADS-Ω™ does not require private model chain-of-thought as a universal control. It governs observable artifacts that can support validation, attribution, and reconstruction.
CONTROL LAYER 04
+
AVC
Action Vector Control
Evaluates decision risk, uncertainty, thresholds, cumulative or compositional exposure, and authority requirements.
Separation valid basis ≠ acceptable risk ≠ authorization.
Why it exists
A decision can be well-formed yet exceed the enterprise's permitted exposure or required authority. AVC™ keeps that distinction explicit before EG™ authorizes execution.
CONTROL LAYER 05
+
EG
Execution Governance
Owns final runtime authorization and enforcement at the Decision Trust Boundary.
DAS™ GRANT · BLOCK · ADJUST · GATE · SUSPEND
Boundary enforcement
Authorization is bound to the specific decision, permitted action, target, tool, policy, authority, constraints, and validity. Unknown, error, timeout, or mandatory dependency failure is not GRANT.
CONTROL LAYER 06
+
TMG
Trace Matrix Grid
Owns runtime evidence continuity and Decision Provenance Receipt assembly where required.
Evidence attributable continuity across the lifecycle.
Privacy-aware evidence
Evidence can preserve references, transformations, metadata, integrity markers, and decision linkage without indiscriminately retaining raw sensitive source information.
CONTROL LAYER 07
+
CCF
Cognitive Containment Field
Owns continuous monitoring, containment trigger evaluation, restriction, recovery, and authorized exit.
Containment actual restriction — not merely alerting.
Why it exists
Authorization can become invalid after material change. CCF™ supplies the control logic for narrowing or suspending affected decision capability and governing recovery.

Five exact Decision Authorization States. No sixth state.

GRANT
Bounded permission to execute within the approved decision, scope, constraints, and validity.
BLOCK
Execution is prohibited.
ADJUST
Only an explicitly authorized modified action may proceed.
GATE
Non-executing state requiring specified human or external authority.
SUSPEND
Non-executing state holding activity pending resolution or reevaluation.

Section 04  ·  The Governing Principle

Velocity of execution must not exceed velocity of accountability.

ADS-Ω™ separates authorization, executed action, and observed outcome. A system may be technically capable of acting while lacking valid decision authority to do so. Confidence is not evidence, and the record is not automatically the truth.

Decision Trust Boundary

Separates a proposed consequential action from an authorized external effect. Before crossing, applicable decision identity, context, decision-basis, risk, authority, constraint, and authorization conditions must be satisfied.

Decision Constraint Envelope

Defines the effective permitted scope of the decision and its execution — including applicable action, target, tool, purpose, authority, data, time, exposure, delegation, and containment constraints.

Unknown, error, timeout, or mandatory dependency failure is not GRANT.
Architecture / Runtime Boundary

ADS-Ω™ is implementation-neutral at the architecture level. Current engineering may use AARM as an external runtime substrate direction, but AARM is third-party, is not an ADS-Ω™ core control layer, and does not own ADS-Ω™ doctrine or authorization semantics.
